# What the other PS4 emulator ports teach (read in 0.9.7 fix 4)

Read: **DolphinPS4** (`DolphinPS4-main.zip`: its Mesa patch, its Dolphin patch, `port/mesa/ac_ps4_drm.c`, its docs), **ps4-switch-emu** (`ps4-switch-emu-main.zip`: its Mesa patches, the host test of its fastmem arena, its scripts and docs) and one `boot-trace` log of ps4-switch-emu on a PS4 Pro, plus the release notes of Eden upstream.
**Nothing here was run.** It is reading: the zips contain source and patches, not builds, and the Eden sources of ps4-switch-emu (its `eden/` folder) are not in the zip. Every "applies / does not apply" below is a judgement from the text, and says what it was judged against.

Licences: DolphinPS4 is GPL-2.0-or-later, ps4-switch-emu is GPL-3.0; this project is GPL-3.0-or-later. Nothing was copied: the ideas were read and what was written here (the menu, `frontend/cpu_clock.h`) is our own code.
ps4-switch-emu's README says Eden does not accept AI-assisted contributions: **nothing from this project should be sent upstream to Eden**.

## 1. GPU hangs (DolphinPS4, on RADV for GCN)

| # | What DolphinPS4 found | Where | Us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The PS4's command processor hangs on `SET_PREDICATION` (BOOL64).** Dolphin's first frame stopped at the first one. Their fix: `radv_emit_set_predication_state()` returns at once, `VK_EXT_conditional_rendering` is not exposed, and the passes RADV itself predicates (fast-clear eliminate) always run (`pred_offset = 0`). | `mesa-ps4.patch`: `radv_cmd_buffer.c`, `radv_physical_device.c`, `meta/radv_meta_fast_clear.c` | **Already ours, from the other side**: patch 0009 removes `VK_EXT_conditional_rendering` before Eden creates the device (found in 0.1.0 as "GPU hangs on GFX7"). This explains why. What patch 0009 cannot reach is RADV's *own* use of predication inside the driver (fast-clear eliminate); the driver is not in this repository, so whether it is predicated there is **not known**. If a hang shows up at the first draw into a freshly cleared image, that is the place to look. |
| 2 | **A draw with a geometry shader wrote past RADV's ES→GS ring into the next buffer, and the system killed the app** (SADX's lines/points). Their fix: no geometry shaders at all (`bSupportsGeometryShaders = false`, lines and points are expanded in the vertex shader). | `dolphin-ps4.patch`: `VulkanContext.cpp` | Eden **cannot** drop geometry shaders (games have them). The same defect shows in the other two projects: ps4-switch-emu's log has `sceGnmSetGsRingSizes(8 MiB, 8 MiB)`, and ours has `GS ring 1048576 B requested -> 16777216 B allocated` (the driver multiplies the ring by 16; `env=ORBIS_GS_RING_SCALE=N` is read by it). Three projects, one defect, one remedy: the 16x is not a guess. It also gives a lead for Mario Maker 2's missing layers (`CHANGELOG` 0.8.1: six pipelines **with a geometry shader (points)** were created at the instant the layers were missing): **a hypothesis, nothing more**; a test is `env=ORBIS_GS_RING_SCALE=32` and a look at the same scene. |
| 3 | **A corrupted command buffer hangs the GPU or faults it, and the system kills the app.** Their winsys walks every IB chain before submitting (`ps4_check_ib_chain`: each IB starts with a packet header, each chain link points into a live mapped buffer); a bad one is dropped (the fences still signal) and written to `ib-corrupt.txt` with the first 48 dwords. They saw "a chained IB full of vertex-like float data" and "something overwrote the first dwords of chained IBs" (`radv_ps4_check_ib_start`). Also a hang report (`gpu-hang.txt`: the PM4 stream walked to see how far the GPU got, with RADV's trace ids) and a physical-overlap check of live buffers. | `port/mesa/ac_ps4_drm.c` | **Driver side, not in this repository.** It is the best protection against "a hang that takes the console down": a submission that would be wrong is not sent. Worth doing in the driver repository (the walk is a few reads per IB). Our `mesa.log` shows a similar flattener (`flattener self-test`) but no such check was seen in it. |
| 4 | **The PS4 Pro in enhanced mode** (its winsys reports Bonaire with 4 shader engines) **hangs the work distributor the same way Hawaii does** without Hawaii's IA/WD workarounds (WD/IA/VGT/PA busy, SC idle, at the first draws). Their fix: `radv_hawaii_like()` (Bonaire with `max_se == 4`) in `radv_get_ia_multi_vgt_param` and after streamout. | `mesa-ps4-pro-hawaii.patch` (ps4-switch-emu) | **Does not apply to this build as it is**: our driver reports 18 CUs / base layout (`mesa.log`: `matches the published 18 CU` and "direct memory pool is 4608 MiB" = base mode). It would matter the day this app declares Pro support (see 2.2): and `docs/EDEN4.md` says Eden4 reported **a GPU hang and a console crash** in Pro mode. |
| 5 | A thread that records Vulkan commands for the video thread (`PS4CommandRecorder`: RADV's per-draw work was 16% of Dolphin's video thread in a draw-heavy game). | `dolphin-ps4.patch` | **Does not apply**: Eden already records on its own Vulkan worker thread (`VulkanWorker` in our profiler table). |
| 6 | Smaller things: `RADV_DEBUG=hang,noumr,syncshaders` and `RADV_PS4_TRACE` (per-draw trace ids read by the hang report), `RADV_PS4_SERIALIZE`/`RADV_PS4_MITIGATE` (ps4-switch-emu's log: `mitigations 0: CP DMA prefetch on, VS late alloc on`). | `ac_ps4_drm.c`, the log | These are switches of **their** driver. Ours is another one (`orbis-drm`: `knobs: TRACE DUMP_SUBMIT CMASK TILE_MODE ARENA_MIB` in `mesa.log`), none of those names appears in its logs: **do not put them in `settings.txt`**, they would do nothing. |

## 2. ps4-switch-emu

**2.1 Fastmem.** Its arena maps a block of **16 KiB** (the PS4 page) only when the four guest 4 KiB pages of that block lie next to each other in the backing memory, aligned, with the same permission; every other block stays reserved-but-unmapped, so an access to it faults and takes the slow path. `tools/fastmem-test` runs the real class against a simulated kernel with 200 000 random Map/Unmap/Protect operations and checks every block after each. Two things for us:
- the **test method** is worth taking for our own arena (patches 0002/0027/0029, 64 KiB chunks): a model of what each page must be, compared after every random operation. Ours has host tests of the ownership rules but not a randomized comparison with a simulated kernel.
- its source (`eden/src/common/host_memory.cpp`) is **not in the zip**, so the arena itself could not be read.
`sceKernelEnableDmemAliasing` is what lets the same memory be mapped twice (their log: "plainly: refused (0x80020010); after sceKernelEnableDmemAliasing (0x0): yes"). This repository's experimental branch already found aliasing works (`docs/TECHNICAL.md`, JIT); the main branch does not use fastmem (`docs/STATUS.md`, tests 25–29), so this changes nothing there.

**2.2 The PS4 Pro runs an app at the base clock unless told otherwise.** Its `package-eden.sh` says: without `ATTRIBUTE 0x00800000` in `param.sfo` ("supports PS4 Pro") a PS4 Pro runs the app at the base PS4's clocks, **CPU 1.6 GHz instead of 2.13**, unless **Boost Mode** is on in the console's system settings. This repository's `package-eden.sh` writes `ATTRIBUTE 0`, and `docs/STATUS.md` reasons with 2.13 GHz; **nobody measured it**. 0.9.7 fix 4 adds one line to `boot.log` (`cpu clock: the time-stamp counter ticks at N MHz ...`, `frontend/cpu_clock.h`): with Boost Mode off and then on, the two logs say whether it matters. Declaring Pro support is **not** proposed here: it changes the GPU (4 shader engines, more direct memory), their own Pro package is marked untested, and Eden4 had a hang and a crash.

**2.3 Large pages, measured on their console** (`boot-trace` log, a PS4 Pro): random reads over 128 MiB cost **146 ns through a 2 MiB-aligned mapping, 188 ns through a misaligned one and 188 ns through 512 KiB pieces**. Their guest RAM is taken in 2 MiB chunks ("RAM chunk N mapped at ..., direct memory 0x..., 2 MiB aligned"). Our lazily committed guest memory is taken in **64 KiB** pieces (patches 0002/0027/0029), so it is in the slow class. 22% less latency on random reads is a lot for a CPU-bound emulator, but 2 MiB chunks hold more memory than 64 KiB ones when the game's memory is sparse, and memory is what Mario Odyssey and Mario Maker 2 run out of. **Not changed.** A console run that counts the committed memory of the same game with both sizes would decide it (0.10 in the roadmap, after the memory problems are closed).

**2.4 Other things in its log**, for what they are worth: it restarts the app for every game ("a fresh process, with all its memory"; this project does it too, `restart_state.txt`); its JIT code caches are 128/80/80/8 MiB taken from flexible memory (ours: direct memory, in 2 MiB steps, patch 0034); it runs 7 cores (`cores 0x7f`); its Eden starts `padtest.nro` in under a second and runs it at 28 fps with 0 GPU draws (the program draws on the CPU), so it is **no comparison** for our `.nro` that stays black.

## 3. Eden upstream

What could be checked: the release notes on `git.eden-emu.dev/eden-emu/eden/releases` (newest found: **v0.2.1, 2026-06-01**). v0.2.0 (2026-05-13) lists: dispatcher, decoder and IR-constructor changes that cut JIT latency; a simplified texture-cache GC; static storage for vertex buffers; **SPIRV-Opt removed** (faster shader compilation); v0.2.0-rc2: JIT and memory performance; rc1: JIT caching and page performance; v0.1.0: far less memory synchronisation and DMA overhead (largest in DMA-heavy games), faster shader compilation on low-end systems, GPU texture unswizzling options.
What could **not** be checked: the commits after this project's pin (`5f142c79`, the commit ProsperoEden uses): the commit pages are closed to automated reading (`robots`), the GitHub mirror gave an error, and the pin's date could not be read. So **which of the above is already in the pin is not known**, except that the pinned tree has no SPIRV-Tools optimiser call in `shader_recompiler` or `video_core` (searched), so that one is not a thing to port.
*(0.9.8 could read the commits after all: see `docs/PS5-PORTS.md` section 3. The paragraph above is what 0.9.7 fix 4 could check.)* Moving the pin is not part of this zip: 39 patches sit on it, most of them in memory and the JIT, which is where upstream changed the most; a new pin is a version of its own (rebase, a CI build, a console run).

## 4. Where this leaves the "console crashes" list

In order of what is cheapest to try on the console, none of it needing a new build beyond this zip:
1. **Boost Mode** (`boot.log` line `cpu clock:`): a CPU speed question that may be worth a quarter more speed, answered by two logs.
2. `env=ORBIS_GS_RING_SCALE=32` against the missing layers of Mario Maker 2 (a hypothesis from section 1.2).
3. For the driver repository: the IB-chain check and the hang report of section 1.3, and whether fast-clear eliminate is predicated (1.1).
4. For a later version: 2 MiB guest-memory chunks measured on Mario Odyssey (2.3), and a randomized arena test (2.1).
